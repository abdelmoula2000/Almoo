## Why your laptop gets so hot
[visual: title]
Is your laptop getting hot, loud, or slowing down after a few minutes? In this video I will show you four fixes, from the easiest to the most advanced, so you can cool it down today without buying anything.

## Step 1: Find what is using your CPU
[visual: terminal]
[code: Get-Process | Sort-Object CPU -Descending | Select-Object -First 5]
First, let's find out what is actually making your laptop work so hard. Open PowerShell and run this command. It lists the five processes that used the most CPU time. If you see something you don't recognize, or an app you are not using, close it from Task Manager.

## Step 2: Give it room to breathe
[visual: steps]
[bullets: Use a hard, flat surface; Keep side vents uncovered; Lift the back slightly]
[short: yes]
This one sounds simple, but it fixes a lot of cases. Laptops pull in cool air from the bottom and the sides. If you use it on a bed, a pillow, or your lap, those vents get blocked. Put it on a desk, and lift the back a little with a stand or even a book.

## Step 3: Clean the fan and vents
[visual: steps]
[bullets: Shut down and unplug; Short bursts of compressed air; Hold the fan so it doesn't spin]
[short: yes]
Dust is the number one reason old laptops overheat. Shut it down completely and unplug the charger. Then blow short bursts of compressed air into the vents. Hold the fan still with a toothpick, because letting it spin too fast can damage it.

## Before you open anything
[visual: tip]
[bullets: Back up your files first; Check your warranty; Never force a clip or screw]
Be careful if you decide to open the bottom cover. Back up your files first, check if this voids your warranty, and never force anything. If you are not comfortable, a local repair shop can clean it for a small price.

## Step 4: Switch to a balanced power mode
[visual: steps]
[bullets: Settings, System, Power; Choose Balanced; Turn on battery saver when unplugged]
Windows can push your processor to full speed all the time. Open Settings, go to System, then Power, and set the power mode to Balanced. Your laptop will stay fast when you need it, but run cooler the rest of the time.
